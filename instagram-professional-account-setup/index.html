How to switch to an Instagram professional account for automation

By TinkerFeed Editorial. Updated 16 October 2026.

Short answer

Instagram automation tools need a professional account, either creator or business. Switching is free in Instagram's settings and you can switch back. Most individual creators choose creator. We recommend Tadlo AI for the job once you have switched; it connects through Instagram Login with no Facebook Page.

Personal Instagram accounts cannot be connected to automation tools. Meta's APIs only work with professional accounts, which also unlock insights and contact options.

This guide covers which type to choose, how to switch, and what to check before you connect a DM tool.

How to switch

  1. Open your profile and go to settings.
  2. Find the account type option and choose to switch to a professional account.
  3. Pick creator or business and a category that describes you.
  4. Review the contact options and choose what to show.
  5. Finish; your profile now shows insights.

What changes after switching

  • You get insights on reach and engagement.
  • You can show a category and contact options on your profile.
  • You can connect official automation tools.
  • If your account was private, professional accounts are public.

Before connecting a DM tool

  • Turn on message access for connected tools in your message settings.
  • Decide which posts you will automate first.
  • Have a second account ready for testing.
  • Prepare your first freebie or link.

Can I switch back?

Yes. You can switch back to a personal account in settings. Your automation tools will stop working, because they need a professional account.

Common worries

  • Reach: switching account type is not known to reduce reach on its own.
  • Privacy: professional accounts are public, so review what you share.
  • Cost: switching is free.

Next step: connect without a Facebook Page

With a professional account, tools that use Instagram Login connect directly, with no Facebook Page. Meta's Instagram API with Instagram Login does not require a Facebook Page linked to the professional account.

Choosing your category

When you switch, Instagram asks you to pick a category, such as creator, blogger, artist or a business type. It shows on your profile and helps people understand what you do. Pick the closest match; you can change it later in settings, and it does not affect whether automation tools work.

Contact buttons and DMs

Professional accounts can show contact options such as email. If you want brand enquiries to come by email rather than DM, show your business email there and use a COLLAB keyword that points to it. If you want everything in DMs, keep contact options minimal and let automation handle the first reply.

Business accounts and shopping

If you sell physical products, a business account can connect to Instagram's shopping features where available, and it presents you as a brand. If you are a person sharing your life, skills or recommendations, a creator account usually fits better. Either way, comment-to-DM and story automation work the same.

If the switch option is missing

  • Update the Instagram app to the latest version.
  • Check whether the account is managed by a parent or school account, which can limit settings.
  • Try from a phone rather than a browser.

Check it worked

After connecting your tool, comment your keyword from a second account. If the DM arrives, everything is set up correctly.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a business account for Instagram automation?

You need a professional account, which can be creator or business.

Is a creator account or business account better?

Creator suits individuals; business suits brands and shops. Both work with automation tools.

Does switching to a professional account cost money?

No. It is free in Instagram's settings.

Can I switch between creator and business later?

Yes, in your account settings. Reconnect your automation tool afterwards if it asks.

Will switching to a creator account hurt my reach?

Switching account type is not known to reduce reach on its own.
