Instagram automation for food creators

By TinkerFeed Editorial. Updated 16 October 2026.

Short answer

Food creators should send the full recipe by comment keyword, collect emails for a weekly recipe list, link ingredients or tools they use, and auto-reply to brand collab enquiries. We recommend Tadlo AI for the job: keyword DMs, product carousels for ingredients and email capture, with unlimited DMs on every plan.

Food reels are fast; recipes are long. Viewers want the quantities, the steps and the substitutions, and "comment RECIPE" is the cleanest way to give them that without cramming the caption.

This playbook covers the food creator's automation setup, from recipe cards to brand deals.

The recipe card

A recipe card should fit on one screen: ingredients with quantities, short numbered steps, time and servings. Host it as a page or PDF that opens on a phone. Add one note on substitutions, since that is the most common follow-up question.

A sample recipe DM

Recipe DM

Here's the full {{dish_name}} recipe! {{recipe_link}}
No {{ingredient}}? Use {{substitute}} instead.
Want a new recipe every Sunday? Reply with your email.

Allergens and safety

  • List common allergens in the recipe card.
  • Do not make health claims about dishes or ingredients.
  • Be clear about cooking temperatures and times where safety matters.

Ingredient and tool links

Viewers often ask where you bought a pan or a spice. A TOOLS keyword that sends a carousel of the items, with affiliate links disclosed, answers that and earns from it. Keep the carousel short and specific to the reel.

Hinglish and regional keywords

If your audience comments in Hinglish, add variants such as "recipe do", "recipe bhejo" and "kaise banaya". For regional dishes, add the dish name in the spellings your audience uses.

Brand collabs

Food brands often reach out through DMs and comments. A COLLAB keyword that sends your media kit and business email keeps those enquiries organised. ASCI's influencer guidelines ask creators in India to label paid, gifted and affiliate content with a clear tag such as "Ad", "Collaboration", "Affiliate" or "Free gift", not buried among hashtags.

Seasonal and festive recipes

Festivals and seasons bring searches for specific dishes. Prepare recipe cards ahead, use a seasonal keyword such as the dish name, and pin the comment. Festive recipe reels are some of the most saved food content, so make sure the DM works before you post.

Build a recipe list

  1. Ask for an email in the RECIPE DM.
  2. Send a weekly recipe email.
  3. Use the list to launch a recipe ebook or cooking class later.

Video recipes vs written cards

Some viewers want to cook along with a video; others want a written card in the kitchen. Link the card in the DM and mention that the full video is on your profile, so both kinds of cook are covered.

Restaurant and home-chef creators

If you also cook for orders or run a cloud kitchen, add an ORDER keyword that sends your menu and ordering link, separate from recipe requests. Keep the two clear, so recipe fans are not pushed into sales and customers find the menu fast.

Measure

Track recipe DMs, emails and tool-link clicks per reel. Recipes that get many DMs but few emails may need a stronger reason to subscribe, such as a weekly plan.

Frequently asked questions

How can food creators use Instagram automation?

To send full recipes by comment keyword, share ingredient and tool links, build a recipe email list and handle brand enquiries.

What should a recipe DM include?

A link to a one-screen recipe card with quantities, steps, time, servings and a substitution note.

Can I send ingredient links in a DM?

Yes, as a carousel. Disclose affiliate links.

Should I put the recipe in the caption or the DM?

A short ingredient list in the caption helps; the full recipe card in the DM gets more comments and builds your email list.

How do food creators get brand deals?

Make it easy: a COLLAB keyword that sends your media kit and business email.
